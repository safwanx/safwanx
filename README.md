## 👋 About me

I build and study trustworthy multimodal AI: models that combine images, video, and other signals, and how they behave once they leave clean benchmarks. What interests me is what an average score hides. When does a model's prediction stop being reliable after a distribution shift, and what does a person still give away after their face has been blurred?

- 💼 Junior ML Engineer at **BestCircle AI**, Dhahran. Predictive maintenance, anomaly detection, and OCR-driven document review for industrial clients
- 🎓 Teaching Assistant with **KAUST Academy**, mentoring SRSI student research from problem formulation to write-up
- 📄 Published at **CVPR Workshops 2026**, with another paper under review at IJCV
- 🔭 Working toward reliable and privacy-aware multimodal systems for healthcare and industry
- 🥏 Away from the screen: Ultimate Frisbee, photography, hiking, board games

## 🔬 Research

**TWASEL at SignEval 2026: Adaptive Multi-Stream Pose Fusion for Continuous Sign Language Recognition**<br>
Sadam Al-Azani, **Safwan Nabeel**, Qasim Al Mahfood, Mohanad Mohamed · *CVPR Workshops 2026*<br>
Dedicated body, hand, and face encoders, adaptive gating that decides how much each stream contributes over time, and Conformer temporal modeling. 16.62% word error rate on the SignEval 2026 test set.<br>
<a href="https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/MSLR/papers/Al-Azani_TWASEL_at_SignEval_2026_Adaptive_Multi-Stream_Pose_Fusion_for_Continuous_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf"><img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Paper-PDF-9c3f2a?style=flat-square" alt="Paper"></a> <a href="https://github.com/sazani/TWASEL-SignLang"><img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Code-GitHub-1e1611?style=flat-square&logo=github" alt="Code"></a>

**Are Crowd Counts Reliable? Post-Hoc Reliability Assessment for Crowd Counting Under Dataset Shift**<br>
**Safwan Nabeel**, Sadam Al-Azani, Muhammad Jabbar · *Under review, IJCV*<br>
A post-hoc framework that predicts per-image error risk for frozen crowd counters. Keeping the 80% of images ranked most reliable cuts mean absolute error by 19 to 68% across seven shift settings.
